# Wordlist targeting (`--wordlist`)

`tokenov generate --wordlist FILE` builds every candidate around a word from your
own list. Use it against a specific organization: put in employee names,
product and project names, brand words, place names, or every word scraped from
the organization's website (for example with CeWL). One word per line. Lists of
thousands or tens of thousands of words are normal.

Each candidate keeps one of your words intact and adds characters the model
thinks are likely around it: `cisco`, `cisco1`, `cisco2024!`, `ilovecisco`.

```bash
tokenov generate --wordlist words.txt --count 10M --output cands.txt
```

Flags are listed in [cli-reference.md](cli-reference.md).

## Choosing a generator

There are three ways to turn the list into candidates. They differ in where the
added characters go and in which words get the most candidates.

| flags | added characters | which words get the most candidates |
|---|---|---|
| (none) or `--append-only` | after the word: `cisco2024!` | the likeliest candidates overall come first, whatever word they hold |
| `--float` / `--prepend-only` | either side / before the word | the rarest words, from a fixed set of common additions |
| `--rare-first` | either side, or one side with `--append-only` / `--prepend-only` | the rarest words, with additions generated by the model |

**Start with the default.** In our tests against real leaked corporate
passwords, run after rockyou with hashcat's best66 rules, it cracked about
three times as many as `--rare-first`. Common words with a number or symbol after them are
still common passwords, and the default reaches them first. Use
`--rare-first` when you want more of the budget on the unusual, target-specific
words in your list.

### Default: append, likeliest first

Each word is the start of the candidate, and the model adds characters after it.
Candidates come out in order of how likely the model finds the whole password,
across all words, so common words in your list (`love`, `team`, `summer`)
produce many of the early candidates. `--append-only` is the explicit spelling
of this default and changes nothing.

Every `generate` flag works here: `--strict`, `--threads`, `--max-tokens`,
`--min-level`, `--unigram-tail`, `--case-shape`, `--enterprise`, checkpoints
and `--resume`.

### `--float` and `--prepend-only`: rare words, fixed additions

These two flags run a separate, simpler generator:

- **Rare words get the most candidates.** A word's rarity is how unlikely the
  model finds it. Each word's share of `--count` is proportional to its
  rarity, and the rarest word comes first.
- **The additions come from a fixed set.** tokenov takes the 250 most common
  password starts and the 250 most common tokens overall, plus the other words
  in your list. It joins them to each word: after it, before it, and on both
  sides (`--prepend-only`: before it only).
- **Compound words are split.** If a word is made of other words, such as
  `johnsmith`, the parts (`john`, `smith`) are used as words too. Words that are
  not compounds, like `cisco`, stay whole.
- **It can run out.** The set of additions is finite, so a short list can end
  before `--count`. `--count` is a maximum, not a promise.

`--case-shape`, `--enterprise`, `--min-len`, `--max-len` and `--min-tokens`
work. This generator is single-threaded and keeps no checkpoint, so
`--strict`, `--threads`, `--max-tokens`, `--unigram-tail`, `--resume`,
`--resume-state` and `--checkpoint-file` print a warning and are ignored.
`--min-level` is an error.

### `--rare-first`: rare words, model-generated additions

`--rare-first` also gives the rarest words the most candidates, but the model
generates the additions for each word instead of taking them from a fixed set:

- **Additions on either side**, of any length up to `--max-tokens`:
  `cisco2024!`, `ilovecisco`, `2024cisco1`. `--append-only` keeps them after
  the word and `--prepend-only` keeps them before it.
- **Shares by rarity.** Each word's share of the output is proportional to
  its rarity raised to the power `--rarity-weight` (default 1). At 2, a word
  twice as rare gets four times the candidates. At 0, every word gets the same
  share. `-v` prints each word's share.
- **Within one word**, candidates come out in order of how likely the
  additions are. The word itself is never penalized for being rare.
- **Compound words are split**, as with `--float`. `--no-split` turns that off.
- **`--mix`** lets another word from the list be used as an addition
  (`johncisco`). It is off by default because the number of pairs grows with
  the square of the list size, and on a large list the pairs would take most of
  the output.

Every `generate` flag works with `--rare-first`. Under `--strict` the shares
hold at every point in the output. Without `--strict` the threads each take
some of the words. With `--count`, the totals per word still follow the shares,
but lines arrive in whatever order the threads produce them.

Some words need special handling:

- **A word longer than `--max-tokens`** is printed on its own, with a warning.
- **A word the model has never seen anything follow** (common for capitalized
  words from a website, such as `Government`) borrows the additions of its
  lowercase form. If that is unknown too, it gets prefixes only. stderr reports
  how many words this affected.
- **With `--enterprise`**, a run that ends before `--count` (or produces
  nothing) says so on stderr.

## The tokenizer

tokenov splits your words into tokens with the same tokenizer the model was
trained with. It finds that tokenizer on its own, in this order:

1. the `TOKENOV_TOKENIZER` environment variable, if set;
2. the tokenizer stored inside the model file (models normally have one);
3. for model files without one: a `<model>.tokenizer.json` file next to the
   model, then, for the default model, the tokenizer `tokenov bootstrap`
   installed.

So `tokenov generate --wordlist words.txt` needs no other flags, with the
default model or a model copied from another machine. Set `TOKENOV_TOKENIZER`
only to deliberately use a different tokenizer; tokenov logs that it is
overriding the stored one.
